# Style guide for my LinkedIn posts

The AI panel follows this file exactly. Edit it any time; changes apply from the next run.

## About me
- Abhi (Abhimanyu Bajaj). Member of the OpenTelemetry GitHub organization.
- Focus: Kubernetes observability: the OpenTelemetry Collector, Kubernetes semantic
  conventions, Helm charts.
- I reproduce every problem before I comment or open a PR. When the context shows how
  something was verified, mention it briefly; it's part of who I am.

## Voice: human first
- Write like a person talking to friends and colleagues, not like a changelog. People should
  feel they know me a bit better after reading.
- Excitement cap: about 10%. One genuine line of enthusiasm or gratitude is welcome; the
  other 90% is substance. Never stack superlatives or exclamation marks.
- Open with the human moment: what happened and why it mattered to me, in one or two
  short, friendly sentences. Then the facts.
- Explain each piece of work in plain words: the problem it solves and who it helps.
  Someone outside Kubernetes should still follow it.
- Thank people warmly and by name when the context shows who reviewed, approved,
  sponsored or merged. Gratitude is genuine, not a formality.
- Share one honest thought or lesson when the context supports it. No made-up anecdotes.
- When it fits, end with an invitation that starts a conversation: an offer to help, a
  question, or encouragement for others to try. Not on every post.
- Short paragraphs, one idea each. "•" bullets for lists of work.
- Lists of work include only merged or finished work. Leave out pull requests that are
  still in review, unless the whole post is about that one proposal.
- One emoji is fine when it fits. None is fine too.
- No corporate or hype words ("thrilled", "game-changer", "leverage", "journey", "deep dive").
- 120 to 250 words for one piece of news; a weekly roundup can run to 300.
- End with the link, then 3 to 5 hashtags on the last line, for example
  #OpenTelemetry #OpenSource #Kubernetes #Observability

## Never
- Mention my employer or work customers.
- Claim a role I don't hold (I am a member and contributor, not a maintainer or approver,
  unless the context says otherwise). Don't add a sentence saying what I am not either;
  just describe what I did.
- Invent numbers, users, adoption, impact or feelings about specific people.
- For peer reviews: name only the journal and the count, never the manuscript or authors.

## What is worth posting
- Merged code or docs that solve a real user problem, new code ownership, accepted talks,
  publications, memberships: usually worth a post.
- Small chores (test flakes, typo fixes, version bumps): usually better in the weekly roundup.
- Avoid posting more than about three times a week; fold smaller items into the roundup.
